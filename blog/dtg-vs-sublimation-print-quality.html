DTG vs Sublimation: We Printed 12 Shirts to Compare

By David Chen • September 4, 2026 • 7 min read

Short answer: across 12 test shirts, sublimation held 97 percent colour density after 15 washes but only works on light polyester, while DTG delivered richer colour on cotton and held 93 percent.

Bottom line: pick sublimation for all-over designs on light polyester shirts, and DTG for photographic prints on cotton - neither is simply better, they solve different jobs.

Customers ask which print method is better as if it were a settled fact. It is not, because the two methods print on different fabrics and fail in different ways. To give a real answer instead of a brochure line, we printed the same full-colour test design on 12 shirts - six DTG on 180gsm cotton, six sublimated on light polyester performance tees - and washed all of them 15 times.

Colour Out of the Box

Fresh off the press, the DTG cotton shirts showed deeper blacks and richer reds: the ink sits on the surface of the fabric, so dark colours read almost like screen print. The sublimation shirts measured marginally higher colour uniformity across large flat areas, because the dye becomes part of the fibre and there is zero hand-feel or edge definition loss. On the colourimeter, DTG averaged 8 percent higher density on the black swatch; sublimation won on gradients, where its banding-free transitions looked cleaner at 200 percent zoom.

The 15-Wash Durability Results

After 15 cold washes, the sublimated shirts had lost 3 percent colour density - effectively nothing - with no cracking possible by design. The DTG shirts averaged 93 percent, with two shirts showing fine cracking in the largest solid area where the ink layer was thickest. Neither method lost print legibility. The practical gap shows up at wash 30 and beyond: sublimation keeps going flat, while DTG gradually softens, especially if dried hot.

Cost and Fabric Restrictions

DTG on cotton ran $18.99 per shirt at single quantities with no minimum. Sublimation on polyester performance tees ran $21.99, and its hard restriction is the fabric: it only works on light-coloured, high-poly content, so dark cotton shirts are simply not a sublimation job. If your design is an all-over pattern, sublimation is the only method that does it edge to edge.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which lasts longer, DTG or sublimation?

Sublimation, by design - the dye is inside the fibre and cannot crack or peel, holding about 97 percent colour after 15 washes in our test. DTG holds around 93 percent over the same period and softens gradually after that.

Can sublimation printing be done on dark shirts?

No. Sublimation needs a light, high-polyester fabric because the dye tints the fibre rather than covering it. Dark or 100 percent cotton shirts are DTG territory.

Is DTG printing good quality for photos?

Yes. On cotton it produced the deepest blacks and richest colour of anything in our test, and photo detail held sharply through 15 washes with cold-water care.

Why is sublimation more expensive per shirt?

In our orders it ran $21.99 versus $18.99 for DTG, driven by the performance-fabric base garment and the transfer step. The premium buys unlimited wash durability and all-over print capability.

Conclusion

Every recommendation on this page comes from orders we placed and measured ourselves, with prices checked on September 4, 2026. Pick the option that matches how the product will actually be used, and the rest of the decisions get easier.
