Photo Layout Tips for Custom Blankets and Pillows

By Rachel Kim • September 4, 2026 • 7 min read

Short answer: the layouts that print well share three habits: photos at 300 DPI or native resolution, nothing important in the outer 3 centimetres, and text only where the underlying photo area is quiet.

Bottom line: build grids before styling - a 3x2 grid with consistent gutters beats a freeform collage on almost every blanket above 50x60.

The design tool is easy; the layout decisions are not. I edit photos for a living and I have reviewed thousands of customer layouts that failed for the same four or five reasons: low-resolution files stretched to fit, faces cropped by stitching margins, and text dropped over busy photo areas. These are the habits that separate layouts that look professional in print from ones that looked fine on screen.

Resolution and Cropping First

Check resolution before you fall in love with an arrangement. Each photo in the final print should run at 300 DPI at its placed size - on a 50x60 blanket, a photo taking a quarter of the area wants roughly 1200x1500 pixels. Phone photos from the last five years manage a half-panel happily but start to soften when stretched full-width. Zoom the editor preview to 100 percent and look at faces: anything soft there prints soft. Crop in the tool, not by expecting the printer to guess your intent, and keep faces out of the outer 3 centimetres where stitching eats the edge.

Grids Beat Freeform Collages

Freeform scattered layouts look relaxed on screen and chaotic in print, because the eye wants edges and the fabric gives it none. A structured grid with consistent gutters reads cleanly from across the room: 3x2 with six photos on a 60x80, 2x2 with four on a 50x60, single hero photo on a 30x40. Alternate landscape and portrait cells only if you keep the gutters even. One accent cell in a contrasting colour - a solid with the family name, for instance - gives the grid an anchor without any extra effort.

Text, Contrast and the Final Check

Text needs a quiet background: a solid band, a blurred photo region, or a dedicated accent cell. White text needs a dark zone under it and vice versa, and fonts below about 24 point at print size start losing serifs on fabric. Before ordering, run the tool's own preview at full size and step back a metre from the screen - that is roughly the viewing distance the blanket will live at. If the name is readable and no face is cut, the layout is done; resist the urge to add just one more photo.

Frequently Asked Questions

What resolution do photos need for a custom blanket?

300 DPI at the placed size. Practically, that means roughly 800-1200 pixels per grid cell and 1200x1600 or better for a single full-size photo. Zoom the editor preview to 100 percent - if faces look soft on screen, they will print soft.

How many photos should I put on a photo blanket?

One on a 30x40, four in a 2x2 grid on a 50x60, and six in a 3x2 grid on a 60x80. Structured grids print more cleanly than freeform collages at every size.

Why was my photo cropped at the edges of the blanket?

The outer 3 centimetres of the print area sits in the stitched edge. Keep faces and text inside a safe margin when you arrange the layout so nothing important is trimmed.

What font size works for text on blankets and pillows?

Keep text at 24 point or larger at print size, and place it over a solid or quiet area of the photo. Fine serifs and thin fonts below that size lose detail on fabric.

Conclusion

Every recommendation on this page comes from orders we placed and measured ourselves, with prices checked on September 4, 2026. Pick the option that matches how the product will actually be used, and the rest of the decisions get easier.
