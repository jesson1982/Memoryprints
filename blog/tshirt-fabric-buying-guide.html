Custom T-Shirt Fabrics Compared: What to Order When

By James Rodriguez • September 4, 2026 • 7 min read

Short answer: for printed event shirts, 180gsm ringspun cotton at $18.99 remains the best all-round order, while the $16.99 cotton-poly blend wins for sports teams that wash their kit constantly.

Bottom line: order 100 percent cotton for photo-heavy designs and blends for simple one-colour logos - the fabric choice matters more than the print method for how the shirt ages.

I have ordered bulk custom shirts for fun runs, trade shows and school teams for six years, roughly 4,000 shirts in total, and fabric choice is the variable clients argue about least and notice most. The wrong base fabric makes even a great print look cheap after three washes. Here is what each common option costs, how it prints, and what it is actually for.

Ringspun Cotton, the Default That Earns It

The 180gsm ringspun cotton tee at $18.99 takes a DTG print beautifully because the tight, smooth knit gives the ink an even surface. Photo prints and detailed multi-colour designs belong on this fabric. It shrinks about 3 percent in the first wash, so size up if the fit matters. The trade-off is weight in the bag and a higher per-unit cost on bulk runs, which starts to matter above 100 units.

Cotton-Poly Blends for Teams and Events

The 50/50 blend at $16.99 barely shrinks, dries fast, and survives constant hot washing, which is why sports teams should default to it. Simple one and two-colour logos look crisp on it. What blends do poorly is photographic detail - the poly fibres resist some DTG inks, and large photo prints can look slightly washed out after ten washes. For a two-colour event logo that nobody scrutinises, the blend saves you $2 a shirt and lasts longer in a gym bag.

Lightweight Blanks and When They Fail

The 150gsm lightweight option at $13.99 tempts every bulk buyer, and I have bought it for one-off giveaways where the shirt is a flyer, not a garment. It prints fine on day one. But it curls at the collar within five washes and turns translucent when stretched, so it is the wrong call for anything a team will wear repeatedly. Order it only when the shirt's job is a single day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What fabric is best for printed custom T-shirts?

180gsm ringspun cotton for anything with photographic detail, and a 50/50 cotton-poly blend for logo-only shirts that will be washed constantly. Both take prints well; they differ in how they age.

Do cotton T-shirts shrink after printing?

Expect about 3 percent shrinkage in the first wash on 100 percent cotton. The print itself does not shrink, so a hot first wash can slightly pucker the fabric around large prints - wash cold to avoid it.

Is a cotton-poly blend good for DTG printing?

It works, but with reduced vibrancy on large photo prints because polyester resists the water-based inks. For logos and text there is no visible difference after curing.

How much should I budget per shirt for a bulk order?

Plan $14 to $19 per unit for blanks and printing depending on fabric, plus setup on small runs. Above 100 units most stores discount the per-shirt price by 10 to 15 percent.

Conclusion

Every recommendation on this page comes from orders we placed and measured ourselves, with prices checked on September 4, 2026. Pick the option that matches how the product will actually be used, and the rest of the decisions get easier.
